# Where did my YouTube subscriptions go?

A couple of days ago, I thought to relax at the end of the day and watch some YouTube videos of my favorite content creators on my phone. Upon opening the app, I was then surprised to see that my subscriptions were nowhere to be found as they weren't in their usual place which was at the bottom bar. I was confused because, for as long as I can remember, the bottom bar or navigation bar contains 5 buttons. These buttons include the homepage, YouTube Shorts, create a video, subscriptions, and a profile button.
The positioning of these buttons made sense and made navigation very easy across the app but now I now had to go on a hunt to find my subscriptions. After closer inspection, I managed to find my subscriptions again under a new tab on the homepage called "subs" right next to the tab that said "home". After getting onto the "subs" tab, I wanted to go back to the homepage, as a video suggested to me caught my attention earlier. Tapping the home button on the navigation bar did not take me back to the main home page as I would have expected it to. It stayed on the "subs" page, as it is now technically part of the home. In order to return to the true home page, I had to tap the "home" tab. The difference can be seen in the image below.

This interaction affected my **Mental Model**, which is how a user expects a website, system, or product to work based on past experiences. My past experience made me expect the subscriptions button at the bottom for ease of access with the same name and not a shortened form, as well as the home button to take me to the home page, but neither of these were as I thought they would be.
A simple solution could be to either inform users of the change and where they can find the moved items with a mini-tutorial. They could also add the ability to opt out of this change and have some sort of legacy navigation bar option for older users. Looking into the desktop version, this change is yet to be implemented, and subscriptions remain where they always were, which is interesting to see.
